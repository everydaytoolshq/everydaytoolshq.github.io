⚡ Section 1 — Confusable Pairs

Drill BOTH sides of every pair. Knowing one date is not enough.

PAIR 1 — 1807 vs 1833: Slavery

1807
Slave Trade abolished
  • Slave Trade Act
  • Abolished buying & selling of enslaved people
  • The TRADE was illegal — but slavery itself still existed
  • Campaigned for by William Wilberforce
1833
Slavery abolished
  • Slavery Abolition Act
  • Enslaved people in the Empire were FREED
  • Slavery itself became illegal — not just the trade
  • £20 million paid to slave owners (not enslaved people)
⚠️ TRAP: Many candidates say '1833' for both — wrong. They are separate events 26 years apart.

PAIR 2 — 1918 vs 1928: Women's Votes

1918
Women over 30
  • Representation of the People Act
  • Women OVER 30 could vote
  • Same year WWI ended
  • First time ANY women could vote in UK
1928
All women over 21
  • Equal Franchise Act
  • Women over 21 — same as men
  • Full equal voting rights for women
⚠️ TRAP: In 1918 it was only over-30s. 'All women' came in 1928. Voting age dropped to 18 in 1969 — different event.

PAIR 3 — 1665 vs 1666: Plague and Fire

1665
Great Plague
  • Bubonic plague hit London
  • ~100,000 deaths in London
  • King Charles II fled London
  • One year BEFORE the Fire
1666
Great Fire of London
  • Started in a bakery on Pudding Lane
  • Burned 4 days — destroyed 13,000 homes
  • St Paul's destroyed — Wren rebuilt it
  • One year AFTER the Plague
⚠️ PLAGUE then FIRE (alphabetical = chronological). One year apart.

PAIR 4 — 55 BC vs 43 AD: Caesar vs Claudius

55 & 54 BC
Julius Caesar
  • Led EXPEDITIONS — not invasions
  • Did NOT conquer Britain
  • Two trips: 55 BC and 54 BC
43 AD
Emperor Claudius
  • The ACTUAL Roman invasion
  • Claudius ordered the conquest
  • Roman rule lasted until ~410 AD
⚠️ TRAP: Never say Caesar invaded Britain — he explored. Claudius invaded in 43 AD.

PAIR 5 — 1707 vs 1800: Two Acts of Union

1707
Kingdom of Great Britain
  • England + Scotland
  • Welsh already united with England since 1536
1800
United Kingdom
  • Great Britain + Ireland
  • Republic of Ireland left in 1922
⚠️ TRAP: 1707 ≠ United Kingdom. United Kingdom came in 1800.

🎯 Section 2 — Wording Traps

Words like ONLY, ALL, ALWAYS, NEVER, PERMANENTLY change everything.

"The House of Lords can BLOCK legislation permanently."
FALSE
Lords can only DELAY — cannot permanently block. 'Permanently' makes it false.
"ALL EU citizens can vote in UK general elections."
FALSE
Only British, Irish and qualifying Commonwealth citizens can vote. EU citizens (except Irish) cannot vote since Brexit.
"The UK has a written constitution."
FALSE
The UK has an UNWRITTEN constitution — not in a single document.
"The Speaker votes on all legislation."
FALSE
The Speaker does NOT vote except to break a tie. Politically neutral.
"Magistrates are always legally qualified."
FALSE
Magistrates are usually community members — unpaid, no legal qualifications required. They are advised by a legal clerk.
"The Monarch is head of the Church of Scotland."
FALSE
Monarch is Supreme Governor of Church of ENGLAND. Church of Scotland is Presbyterian — Monarch is an ordinary member there.
"Wales has its own established church."
FALSE
Church in Wales was disestablished in 1920. Only England has an established church.
"The PM must be a Member of the House of Lords."
FALSE
PM must be a member of the elected House of Commons.
"Jury service is optional in the UK."
FALSE
Jury service is a civic duty — you can be legally required to serve.
"The Scottish Parliament can make laws on ALL matters."
FALSE
Some powers are reserved to Westminster — defence, foreign policy, immigration, national benefits.
"The National Trust is a government organisation."
FALSE
The National Trust is a charity founded in 1895 — independent of government.
"People in the UK are free to practise any religion."
TRUE
Freedom of religion is a fundamental UK right. All religions are free to practise.

🔍 Section 6 — 'Which Statement is Correct?' Drill

One word is wrong. Train your eye to spot it instantly.

✗ The Magna Carta established that the King was above the law.
✓ The Magna Carta established that the King was subject to the law.
✗ The House of Lords is the elected chamber of Parliament.
✓ The House of Commons is the elected chamber of Parliament.
✗ The UK has a written constitution set out in a single document.
✓ The UK has an unwritten constitution drawn from Acts, common law and conventions.
✗ The Slave Trade Act 1807 freed all enslaved people in the Empire.
✓ The Slavery Abolition Act 1833 freed enslaved people in the Empire.
✗ The Church of Scotland is headed by the Monarch as Supreme Governor.
✓ The Church of England is headed by the Monarch as Supreme Governor.
✗ The Supreme Court was created in 1999.
✓ The Supreme Court was created in 2009.
✗ Women over 21 were given the right to vote in 1918.
✓ Women over 30 were given the right to vote in 1918.
✗ Julius Caesar successfully invaded and conquered Britain in 55 BC.
✓ The Roman invasion began under Emperor Claudius in 43 AD.
✗ The NHS was founded in 1948 by Winston Churchill.
✓ The NHS was founded in 1948 by Aneurin Bevan.
✗ Wales is represented in the Union Flag by the red dragon.
✓ Wales is not represented in the Union Flag.
✗ The voting age was reduced to 18 in 1928.
✓ The voting age was reduced to 18 in 1969.
✗ Scotland has a jury of 12 in criminal trials.
✓ Scotland has a jury of 15 in criminal trials — and 3 possible verdicts.
✗ The Act of Union 1707 created the United Kingdom.
✓ The Act of Union 1707 created the Kingdom of Great Britain.
✗ The Prime Minister lives at 11 Downing Street.
✓ The Prime Minister lives at 10 Downing Street. (Chancellor lives at No. 11)
✗ Magistrates are legally qualified judges who receive a salary.
✓ Magistrates are usually community members who work unpaid, without legal qualifications.

🎲 Section 7 — Rarest Questions (appear 1 in every 15–20 tests)

What does 'Habeas Corpus' mean?Latin for 'you shall have the body' — gives prisoners the right to a court hearing.
Who was Capability Brown?Lancelot 'Capability' Brown (1716–1783) — most famous landscape gardener; designed over 170 gardens.
Who were the Puritans?Strict Protestants who wanted to 'purify' the Church of England; supported Parliament in the Civil War.
What is the West Lothian Question?The anomaly where Scottish MPs can vote on English-only matters at Westminster, but English MPs cannot vote on devolved Scottish matters.
What are the Lords Spiritual?The 26 bishops of the Church of England who sit in the House of Lords.
What is a by-election?An election to fill a vacant Commons seat between general elections (e.g. when an MP dies or resigns).
When did the Church of England disestablish in Wales?1920 — the Church in Wales was disestablished. Wales has no established church.
What was the Rump Parliament?What remained after Cromwell purged MPs opposed to trying Charles I — later dissolved by Cromwell himself in 1653.
What are the Crown Dependencies?Jersey, Guernsey and the Isle of Man — NOT part of the UK but self-governing territories under the Crown.
What is the Electoral Register?A list of all eligible voters. Must be registered to vote. Also used to select jurors.
At what Olympics do UK nations compete as one team?The Olympics — as 'Team GB'. But in football World Cups etc. each nation competes separately.
What is common law?Law based on court decisions and legal precedent — forms part of the UK's unwritten constitution.
What is the Petition of Right (1628)?Presented to Charles I — confirmed no imprisonment without cause, no taxes without Parliament's consent.
What was the Heptarchy?The seven Anglo-Saxon kingdoms: Northumbria, Mercia, East Anglia, Wessex, Kent, Essex, Sussex.
