About

About The Yarn Logic Pro

The Yarn Logic Pro is a free browser-based tool designed for knitters and crocheters who want accurate yardage estimates before they head to the yarn shop. Whether you're planning a chunky cable-knit sweater, a delicate lace shawl, or a cosy baby blanket, the calculator takes your specific project size, stitch texture, gauge, and ease preferences into account.

Unlike basic estimators that apply a single flat figure per project type, The Yarn Logic Pro uses a multi-factor model. The Stitch Geometry system recognises that cables and bobbles can use up to 45% more yarn than plain stockinette — a difference that can mean several extra skeins on a large adult garment. The gauge correction squares the ratio between pattern gauge and your personal tension, which is mathematically correct and significantly more accurate than a linear adjustment.

A built-in Buttonhole Spacing Calculator rounds out the tool for garment makers, distributing stitches in an alternating pattern so button bands look professional. All calculations happen entirely in your browser — nothing is sent to any server.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is the yardage estimate?
Very accurate for standard projects. The calculator uses a multi-factor model: base yardage by size, Stitch Geometry texture multipliers, squared gauge correction, ease multipliers, and a safety buffer. For unusual constructions or heavily modified patterns, add extra buffer using the slider.
Why does crochet need more yarn than knitting?
Crochet uses a 1.3× multiplier because the craft typically consumes around 30% more yarn than an equivalent knitted fabric. This is due to the taller stitch height and the way yarn wraps around the hook multiple times per stitch.
What is the Stitch Geometry Tax?
Stitch Geometry is the system that accounts for how texture complexity changes yarn consumption. Baseline stockinette uses 1.0× (no extra). Volume textures like garter or ribbing use 1.1×. Structural stitches like cables use 1.25×. Sculptural work like bobbles and popcorn stitches use 1.45×.
How does gauge correction work?
The calculator divides your pattern gauge by your personal gauge and squares the result. If you knit tighter than the pattern (more stitches per inch), your gauge factor will be below 1, reducing the estimate. If you knit looser, it increases. Squaring the ratio captures the two-dimensional nature of fabric area accurately.
What does the buttonhole alternating pattern mean?
When you have leftover stitches that don't divide evenly into gaps, the calculator distributes the extra stitches in an alternating pattern — for example, 8, 7, 8, 7, 8 — instead of grouping all larger gaps at one end. This gives button bands a more professional and balanced appearance.
Can I use this for yarn substitution?
Yes. Enter the length per ball of your substitute yarn in the main calculator fields, and enter the original pattern yarn's length per ball in the Yarn Substitution field. The calculator will tell you how many balls of each you need.
