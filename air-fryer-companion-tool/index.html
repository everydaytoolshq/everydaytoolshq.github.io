FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does the air fryer need a different temperature than an oven?
Air fryers circulate hot air at high speed in a small cavity, which transfers heat much more efficiently than a conventional oven. You typically need to reduce temperature by around 15–25°C (25–45°F) and reduce time by 20–30% to avoid overcooking.
Is it safe to cook chicken wings from frozen?
Yes — the Frozen Food Converter is calibrated for this. For food safety, always ensure poultry reaches an internal temperature of at least 74°C (165°F). Always use a probe thermometer for poultry.
Why do I need to shake or flip during cooking?
Air circulates from above in most air fryers, meaning the bottom of your food can cook more slowly. Shaking or flipping ensures even browning and crispiness on all sides, especially for fries, nuggets, and anything breaded.
Does crunch level actually make a difference?
Yes. The Crispiness Optimizer adjusts temperature, cooking time, shake frequency, and oil recommendation based on your crunch target. These four variables working together have a significant effect on the final texture.
Can I cook multiple foods together?
Yes — use the Multi-Food Sync Timer to stagger your start times so everything finishes at once. The key is to never overcrowd the basket; if in doubt, cook in batches for better airflow and crispier results.
Should I use oil in an air fryer?
A light spray of oil makes a significant difference for crispiness on vegetables, plain chicken, and potato-based foods. Breaded or battered foods usually don't need oil. Use a spray bottle for even, light coverage — never pour oil directly into the basket.
